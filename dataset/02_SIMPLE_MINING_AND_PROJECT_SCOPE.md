# Simple Mining and Project Scope

> Source type warning: statements about Simple Mining's fleet and technology are company claims unless independently corroborated. Cascade-specific descriptions from press coverage are not substitutes for final plans or permits.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-09-10

## What Simple Mining publicly describes itself as

Simple Mining's current documentation describes an Iowa-based company focused on selling, hosting, and repairing Bitcoin miners. Its hosting materials describe a business model in which customers place mining equipment at company-operated facilities and the hosting service provides power infrastructure, cooling, networking, security, monitoring, facility operations, and standard maintenance.

Current public company pages are not perfectly synchronized: the hosting page advertises 11 Iowa data centers and more than 150 MW under management, while an August 2026 company timeline described 10 active facilities and 150 MW. This should be treated as a company-page timing/count difference, not silently reconciled.

## Relevant operating examples

Simple Mining's Site 8 page describes a 50+ MW hydro-cooled location hosting more than 10,000 miners. That example demonstrates that Simple Mining has experience with liquid/hydro cooling at meaningful scale, but it does **not** establish the engineering design, water use, or noise profile of the proposed Cascade site.

The company's cooling and water articles say its current fleet uses engineered airflow and closed-loop cooling approaches and makes specific low-water-use claims. Those claims are useful evidence of the company's stated operating model, but an independent project review should verify Cascade's exact cooling loop, external heat rejection, makeup-water needs, coolant, peak conditions, and metering.

## Cascade proposal as publicly reported

March 2026 trade reporting described the proposed Cascade project as roughly four acres with around 40 containerized cryptocurrency-mining units of roughly 160 square feet apiece. That description differs materially from the conventional hyperscale data-center campuses frequently cited in national debates.

Accordingly, comparisons should match at least these variables before being treated as useful evidence:

- electrical load;
- physical footprint;
- containerized vs. building design;
- workload type;
- cooling architecture;
- external heat rejection;
- water source and use;
- generator/UPS design;
- distance to residences;
- applicable noise limits;
- climate;
- utility arrangement;
- final buildout.

## Questions that remain open for Cascade

The public materials reviewed do not establish a final answer for:

1. initial and maximum electrical load;
2. exact number and type of mining/computing units at buildout;
3. whether the facility will be restricted to cryptocurrency mining or could later support other compute workloads;
4. exact cooling-system and dry/adiabatic heat-rejection design;
5. project-specific water use;
6. project-specific acoustic model;
7. number/type/testing schedule of generators;
8. permanent FTE count and salary ranges;
9. requested incentives and expected taxable value;
10. ownership/operator structure and decommissioning responsibility.
